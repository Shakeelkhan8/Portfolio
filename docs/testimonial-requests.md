# Collecting testimonials

How to get genuine testimonials onto the portfolio. Only real words from a
real person, published with their permission, go on the site. Nothing here is
published until someone has written it and agreed to how they're named.

## Who to ask

| Person | Why they're credible | Best channel | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A manager or lead at Hum Network Limited (Sep 2023 to Oct 2025) | Saw the reporting automation and its 100+ hours a month saved | LinkedIn recommendation | Former employer: safe to ask |
| Colleagues from Hum Network | Worked with you day to day | LinkedIn recommendation | |
| Upwork clients | Paid for the work; reviews are verified by Upwork | Ask permission to quote the existing review | Shown with "Verified on Upwork" |
| The ISP management system client (2023) | Ran the system in production | Email or WhatsApp | |
| Elityx colleagues | Know the dispatch platform work best | Only once you're comfortable they know you're looking | Current employer: asking may signal a job search |

LinkedIn recommendations are the strongest option for colleagues: they're
public, tied to a real profile, and anyone can check them.

## Message to send

Adjust the first line for each person. Keep it short; people say yes to small,
specific asks.

> Hi [first name], I'm updating my portfolio and would really value a short
> testimonial from you about working together on [project or team].
>
> Two or three sentences in your own words is perfect. If it helps, these are
> the things I'd most like someone to speak to (pick any, or none):
>
> - how I approach backend and Laravel work
> - building SaaS systems: architecture, databases, multi-tenancy
> - how I investigate and solve problems
> - communication and reliability
>
> Could you also confirm how you'd like to be named: full name or initials,
> your role, and your company (or "Client" if you'd rather not name it)?
> I'll show you exactly how it will look before it goes live.
>
> Thank you, [your name]

For a LinkedIn recommendation, send the same message through LinkedIn's
"Ask for a recommendation", then ask permission to quote it on the site.

## Your four drafts (topics, not text to sign)

Kept as notes on what each kind of testimonial could cover. Don't send these
for someone to approve as their own words: quotes written by you read alike,
and a reader notices. If someone asks for a starting point, share the one that
fits and ask them to rewrite it so it sounds like them.

- **Backend engineering:** understands Laravel and backend engineering; thinks
  through technical problems; writes maintainable code; considers long-term
  reliability, not only the immediate requirement.
- **SaaS architecture:** understands what makes SaaS hard to scale; backend
  architecture, database design and multi-tenant systems.
- **Problem-solving and reliability:** a systematic approach; investigates
  carefully; finds practical solutions; takes ownership of the result.
- **Professionalism and communication:** dependable; communicates clearly;
  understands requirements; thoughtful about technical decisions.

## Before publishing: consent checklist

- [ ] The text is theirs (or their edit of a starting point), and they approved the final wording
- [ ] They confirmed the name format (full name or initials)
- [ ] They confirmed role and company as shown, or chose "Client"
- [ ] They agreed it can appear on the public website
- [ ] You know where it can be verified (Upwork review, LinkedIn recommendation, or their written approval saved somewhere you can find)

## Publishing it

1. Admin → Testimonials → New.
2. Author name, role, and company as they approved. For an Upwork review, end
   the company with `(verified on Upwork)`, e.g. `Client, Mar 2026 (verified on Upwork)`:
   the site turns that into a "Verified on Upwork" badge linking to your
   profile. `(verified on LinkedIn)` shows a badge without a link.
3. Turn on **Visible** and **Featured** (the home page shows featured ones).
4. Admin → Settings → "Refresh public pages".

With two or more testimonials, the home section's heading switches to
"Trusted by people I've worked with." automatically. Never add a rating
unless it is the actual platform rating for that review.
